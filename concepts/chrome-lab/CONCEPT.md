# CHROME LAB — concept notes

**Idea.** The site is a liquid-metal showroom you travel through. One fixed Three.js canvas never unmounts: a mercury blob (hero) buds into orbiting droplets, they gather into the emblem ring and star, and the camera dives through that ring into a chrome tunnel of product-photo planes, then exits to a re-pooled blob. Below the tunnel the scene stays lit: the ring returns face-on behind Worldwide, and the page ends on an unveiled finale where the blob re-forms into the emblem (blob + tilted ring + orbiting star) above a giant chrome footer mark. Scroll position drives everything, so scrolling up plays it in reverse.
**What makes it distinct.** The product showcase is rendered in the scene, not in the DOM: curved photo planes (CDN textures) on a weaving path with a mirror floor, chrome hoops and a light at the tunnel end. Photos are monochrome until you point at them. Scroll velocity bends and ripples the planes, splits their RGB, widens the FOV and agitates the blob. Type is huge tight chrome-gradient Archivo (variable width) with a JetBrains Mono instrument layer: vertical HUD with chapter, depth in metres, scroll velocity and a live Regina clock. The 17-piece running order is curated (dark model shots first, clothes alternating with chrome close-ups, gift card last).
**Key interactions.** The tunnel settles: when scrolling pauses it eases onto the nearest piece, a small nudge steps to the next one, and scrolling past the first or last piece leaves the tunnel (never a trap). Each focused piece is placed in the camera's own frame, so it sits fully on screen on phones and portrait tablets. Planes tilt toward the pointer, grow and turn colour on hover, and open the product view on click or tap. The focused piece upgrades to a screen-sized texture (up to 1600px, sized from device pixels x zoom) while the rest stream light ones, and textures far behind the camera are released. A caption card, a numbered rail (pointer devices), and a "Skip to archive" pill (also on phones) are laid over the scene. The filterable and sortable archive grid, product view (gallery, option chips, sold-out state, price + Add to bag pinned to the bottom on phones, `/cart/<variant>:1` link; a sold-out variant has no link at all), worldwide list from the store data, FAQ, newsletter, contact links and footer all work as before. Prices are `CA$` via Intl with CAD notes.
**Fallbacks and a11y.** No WebGL, a failed module, a lost GL context that does not come back, or `prefers-reduced-motion` gives a plain DOM card grid for the gallery (reduced motion keeps a still hero blob, no glints or grain). If `main.js` itself never runs, the loader lifts on its own after 6.5s (CSS) and store links still carry their hrefs. With 3D on, a visually hidden list of real links is in tab order; focusing one scrolls the scene to it and shows a visible label, Enter opens the product. Keyboard focus rings, `inert` background and focus trap in the product view, alt text everywhere, 44px touch targets, no horizontal scroll from 320 to 2200 wide.
**Performance.** Pixel ratio is capped (2, or 1.5 on phones), self-lowers when frames are slow and climbs back (twice at most); on the lowest rung grain and the chrome glint are switched off. The glint animates only on headings in view and pauses while scrolling. Rendering pauses when the tab is hidden and drops to every third frame behind the product view.
**Known limits.** Needs a GPU for the full effect; frame rate was only tested on software WebGL, so mid-range phones and integrated-GPU laptops still need a real-device pass. The gallery shows 17 of the 23 products (the pale ring-on-card photo, other rings and the sold-out necklace stay in the archive grid); fonts are self-hosted latin subsets; the 3D module needs an http(s) origin (it is skipped on `file://`, which falls back to the DOM grid). `assets/emblem-a.webp` and `wordmark-a.webp` are alpha versions of the originals made for this concept; the original wordmark art is only 700px wide and is clipped at its right edge, so the giant footer mark is set in live chrome type and the raster wordmark is used only at small sizes (nav, footer row). A clean vector or 1400px+ export of the wordmark would let it return as the big footer mark.
